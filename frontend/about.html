About AgroScan AI

AgroScan AI is an independent agricultural technology project that brings together soil-data tools, AI-assisted farming guidance, agricultural learning resources and farm-management features in one web platform.

The public website includes AgroPedia learning pages, AgroAwareness resources, AgroScheme information and AgroShop. Account features provide tools such as saved farm records, soil assessments, chat and analytics.

How AgroScan guidance works

The soil recommendation workflow accepts user-provided measurements such as soil pH and moisture, with optional temperature, location and desired crop information. It combines a rule-based result with an AI-generated response. The quality of the output depends on the accuracy and completeness of the information entered.

AgroScan outputs are informational decision-support guidance. They are not a laboratory soil test, a guaranteed crop-yield forecast, a pesticide prescription or a substitute for advice from a qualified local agricultural professional.

Accuracy, sources and limitations

Agricultural conditions vary by crop, soil, season, district and farm practice. Before applying fertiliser, pesticide or irrigation recommendations, compare the guidance with a current soil laboratory report and local agricultural advisories. Government programmes and eligibility rules can change; confirm details with the official source before applying.

  • Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) — national agricultural research and extension information.
  • Soil Health Card portal — official soil-health programme information and farmer services.
  • India Meteorological Department agricultural services — agrometeorological services and advisories.
  • ICAR institutes directory — research institutes and agricultural institutions.

Project independence

AgroScan AI is an independent project. It is not an official government portal and does not claim affiliation with ICAR, the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, the India Meteorological Department or the Soil Health Card programme.
